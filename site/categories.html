Research Taxonomy

Eleven editorial shelves organize the BlogrXiv index. Each shelf below states its research scope, its boundary with neighboring shelves, and the topic tags used for retrieval.

Editorial method

How we classify

BlogrXiv is a curated, source-linked index, not a submission feed. Before a post reaches a shelf, an editor reviews it against five dimensions. A post that fails on technical contribution or specificity is not indexed at all; a post that passes is assigned to exactly one category, with secondary themes carried by topic tags.

  1. Technical contribution

    Does the post teach a mechanism, method, system behavior, or research lesson beyond what the announcement or abstract already says?

  2. Specificity

    Does it provide enough evidence, implementation detail, or measurement to support technical judgment rather than generic commentary?

  3. Source quality

    Are the author, lab, venue, or community context identifiable and credible, with a stable canonical URL?

  4. Reusability

    Will the post remain useful to a technical reader after the immediate release cycle has passed?

  5. Taxonomic fit

    Can the post be placed on exactly one shelf below and described with searchable topic tags?

In scope

  • Research blogs and lab essays that explain how a method or system works
  • Technical notes on experiments, evaluations, failure modes, and trade-offs
  • Conference blog-track articles with independent technical narrative
  • Engineering write-ups with measurements, ablations, or design rationale
  • Reflections on research practice with transferable, concrete lessons

Out of scope

  • Lab or personal homepages and GitHub organization indexes
  • Product landing pages and marketing announcements
  • Shallow release summaries that restate a changelog
  • Paper-only pages that add no narrative beyond the paper itself
  • News aggregation, link roundups, and leaderboard commentary

Popularity, recency, and affiliation with a prominent organization are never sufficient on their own. A widely shared post from a well-known lab is indexed only if it clears the same five dimensions as any other candidate. The intake paths and review states are documented in the editorial guidelines.

The eleven shelves

Every indexed entry is assigned to exactly one shelf. When a post spans two areas, the boundary note on each card records which shelf takes precedence.

01 / 11

Foundation Model

How general-purpose models are built and measured: pretraining objectives, architecture design, scaling laws, data curation and mixtures, optimization stability at scale, and emergent-capability analysis. Training-run reports and open-weight release documentation belong here when they explain recipe decisions rather than only announce results.

BoundaryPost-training behavior, prompting, and reasoning analysis belong in LLM & MLLM. Serving, quantization, and inference systems belong in Efficient AI. Release commentary without recipe detail belongs in Frontier.

02 / 11

LLM & MLLM

The behavior and adaptation of language models and multimodal language models after pretraining: instruction tuning, preference optimization, reasoning and chain-of-thought analysis, in-context learning, and capability evaluation. Writing here probes what a model does and why, through benchmark design, failure analysis, and post-training recipes.

BoundaryTraining-run and architecture reports belong in Foundation Model. Perception-centric vision-language modeling belongs in Multimodal Model. Multi-step tool-driven autonomy belongs in AI Agents.

Post-Training Reasoning Instruction Tuning Evaluation

03 / 11

Multimodal Model

Models that connect perception to language: vision-language and audio-language architectures, cross-modal alignment and fusion, visual grounding, video understanding, and unified any-to-any modeling. Posts here examine how non-text signals are encoded, aligned with text representations, and evaluated on perception-heavy tasks.

BoundaryText-first reasoning and post-training analysis belong in LLM & MLLM. Image and video synthesis belongs in Visual Generation. Action-conditioned prediction belongs in World Model.

Vision-Language Cross-Modal Alignment Video Understanding Grounding
Browse Multimodal Model entries
04 / 11

Visual Generation

Generative modeling of images and video: diffusion and flow-matching methods, autoregressive visual generation, controllable synthesis and editing, personalization, and the evaluation of generated media. Coverage stays at the level of samplers, guidance, conditioning mechanisms, and fidelity-diversity trade-offs rather than showcase galleries.

BoundaryVideo generation used as an interactive, action-conditioned simulator belongs in World Model. Perception, captioning, and understanding of visual inputs belong in Multimodal Model.

Diffusion Video Synthesis Controllable Editing Generative Evaluation
Browse Visual Generation entries
05 / 11

World Model

Learned models of environment dynamics: interactive video-world simulators, latent dynamics for model-based reinforcement learning, physical and spatial reasoning, and vision-language-action models for robotics. Posts here treat prediction of state transitions—what happens next under an action—as the central object of study, whether the substrate is pixels, latents, or explicit physics.

BoundaryPassive video synthesis without action conditioning belongs in Visual Generation. Software agents that act through tools and APIs rather than learned dynamics belong in AI Agents.

Learned Simulators Model-Based RL Robotics & VLA Physical Dynamics
Browse World Model entries
06 / 11

AI Agents

Systems in which models plan and act over multiple steps: tool calling and orchestration, coding and computer-use agents, browser automation, memory and context management, multi-agent coordination, and agent evaluation harnesses. Emphasis falls on the scaffolding—planning loops, environment interfaces, safeguards, and long-horizon reliability—rather than the base model itself.

BoundarySingle-turn reasoning and prompting studies belong in LLM & MLLM. Agents grounded in learned environment dynamics or embodied control belong in World Model.

Tool Use Coding Agents Computer Use Agent Evaluation
Browse AI Agents entries
07 / 11

Efficient AI

The systems layer of modern AI: inference and serving stacks, attention and KV-cache optimization, quantization, sparsity and distillation, distributed and memory-efficient training, custom kernels, and hardware-aware design. Posts here argue with measurements—latency, throughput, memory, cost—and explain where the reported gains actually come from.

BoundaryScaling analyses tied to pretraining recipes belong in Foundation Model. Efficiency techniques evaluated chiefly for their safety or robustness effects belong in Trustworthy AI.

Inference Systems Quantization Distributed Training Kernels & Hardware
Browse Efficient AI entries
08 / 11

Trustworthy AI

Making model behavior legible and dependable: alignment methods and their limits, mechanistic and representational interpretability, hallucination and robustness analysis, red teaming and jailbreak studies, safety evaluation, monitoring, and the security of deployed systems. Posts here characterize failure modes precisely and connect interventions to measurable behavioral change.

BoundaryGeneral capability benchmarking belongs in LLM & MLLM. Evaluation methodology proposed for the field at large belongs in Research Craft. Policy commentary without technical analysis is out of scope for the index.

Alignment Interpretability Red Teaming Safety Evaluation
Browse Trustworthy AI entries
09 / 11

Research Craft

The methodology layer of AI research: benchmark and evaluation design, statistical rigor and error analysis, experiment infrastructure, reproducibility standards, and essays on how the field measures progress. Posts here aim to improve how research is done and judged across the community, independent of any single model family.

BoundaryFirst-person accounts of the research workflow—writing, reviewing, advising, career habits—belong in How to Research. Evaluations of one specific system belong on that system's shelf.

Evaluation Methodology Benchmark Design Statistical Rigor Reproducibility
Browse Research Craft entries
10 / 11

Frontier

Analytically grounded coverage of frontier movement: what new model releases actually change, open-weight ecosystem shifts, lab technical roadmaps, compute and data constraints, and deployment-facing capability assessment. Posts must read releases critically—comparing measured capabilities against claims—rather than relaying announcements.

BoundaryTechnical documentation of a release's training recipe belongs in Foundation Model. Product launch announcements and press-style coverage are excluded from the index entirely.

Model Releases Open-Weight Ecosystem Lab Roadmaps Capability Assessment

11 / 11

How to Research

First-person writing on the practice of doing research: paper writing and rebuttal strategy, developing research taste, experiment planning and lab-notebook habits, writing dependable research code, reproduction studies, and reflections on advising and scientific careers. The test for this shelf is transferable experience: a reader should leave with habits they can apply to their own work.

BoundaryMethodology proposed for the field at large—benchmarks, evaluation design, measurement standards—belongs in Research Craft.
